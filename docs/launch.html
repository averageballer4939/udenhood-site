Your coin

Launch a coin

Paste your repo and the coin fills itself. Three clicks follow: log in, sign, launch. Every cost is shown first.

Open Launch and paste your repo: Udenhood fills the coin from it, then three clicks do the rest, Log in, Sign on GitHub and Launch the coin.

The three steps

  1. Paste your repo

    Any public GitHub repo you can commit to. Udenhood reads its name, its description and its owner's picture, and fills the coin with them.

  2. Check the coin

    Name, ticker, text and image are there to read and change. Links, first buy, creator tax opens the rest: all of it is optional. What each one costs.

  3. Log in and sign

    Log in, then Sign on GitHub: one file in your repo names your wallet and the coin. How to sign.

Then press Launch the coin on the ticket.

Fixed at launch. The page says it at step 2: “Filled from your repo. Change what you want now: after launch nothing can be edited.” Read every field twice.

Your browser keeps the draft. The form is saved in this browser as you type, so a trip to GitHub does not lose it. It is erased once the coin is live.

Why the repo line? A coin's proof card starts from the coin: it reads that line to know which repo to check. What a coin page reads.

The ticket

The right side of the page follows the form. Five lines light up, the costs are read from the chain, and the note under Launch the coin names the next thing to do, for one “Sign your repo on GitHub.”

When everything is in place the note reads “Ready. Nothing is sent before you confirm in your wallet.”

What happens when you press

  1. The launch is replayed first

    Udenhood reads Pons's terms again and replays your exact transaction against the live chain, without sending it. It checks that the coin about to be created is the one your repo signed. If the replay fails, nothing is sent and the note says why.

  2. “Confirm in your wallet”

    Your wallet shows the transaction: sent to Pons, worth the launch fee plus your first buy. It is the only signature Udenhood asks for.

  3. “Sent to Robinhood Chain”

    The page waits for the receipt. See the transaction opens it on the chain explorer.

  4. “Confirmed on chain”

    The page reads the coin's address in the receipt and shows “Your coin is live”.

After the launch

  • Open its page shows the coin and its proof card on Udenhood.
  • Trade on Pons opens the coin on Pons's own site. Udenhood does not trade.
  • Owner is already on. The last screen says “Signed and launched”: your repo named this coin before it existed. There is nothing more to commit.
  • One thing left for the Coins list. Add the topic udenhood to your repo, in its About settings. Open your repo takes you there. How the list is built.

When it does not go through

LineWhat it means
“This wallet holds 0.0003 ETH. The launch needs about 0.0006 ETH.”the wallet cannot pay the total. Nothing is sent
“You closed the request in your wallet.”you refused the transaction. Nothing was sent
“The coin address changed since you signed. Sign your repo again.”the coin about to be created is not the one the file names. Nothing was sent
“Pons has paused launches.”read on Pons's contract. Nobody can launch until Pons reopens
“Pons only accepts approved wallets right now.”read on Pons's contract for your wallet
“Not confirmed yet.” after 90 secondssent, and no receipt so far. It is not a failure: keep the page open, or follow the transaction before you try again
“The chain refused the transaction. No coin was created. The network fee was spent.”the receipt says the transaction failed on chain

A page reloaded while it waits picks the transaction up again and goes on waiting for its receipt.

What launching does not do

  • No way back. Name, ticker, text, image and creator tax are fixed at launch.
  • No promise. The page says it under the button: a coin can go to zero.
  • No contract of ours. The coin is created by Pons v2 contracts, which are unaudited. The chain and Pons.
  • No light for free. Launching lights Owner. History, Running and Shipping still read the repo.
